# Product Brief

## One-sentence pitch

**A conversational sandbox where a persistent, fully voiced little person lives inside a jar and reacts intelligently to anything the player drops in.**

## Experience

The jar sits close to the screen like a tiny living diorama. The resident notices the player, remembers prior treatment, develops opinions, asks for things, and makes plans. Sliding the lid sideways exposes a search field. Submitting a phrase closes the field, restores the lid, and drops the interpreted object through the mouth of the jar.

The pleasure is not collecting objects. It is discovering consequences:

- A hungry resident eats an apple, but a suspicious one may inspect it first.
- Dog feces produces disgust, avoidance, complaints, and a hygiene problem.
- A rubber ball invites play.
- A bat plus a ball unlocks striking, accidental injury, and escape-oriented uses.
- Water plus cloth allows cleaning.
- Rope plus a sharp edge allows cutting.
- A ladder, lever, adhesive, or repeated impact may become part of an escape plan.
- A deceptive resident may conceal progress, flatter the player, or misuse an apparently harmless gift.

## Core loop

1. Observe the resident’s behavior, speech, needs, and environment.
2. Talk, listen, or infer what they want.
3. Slide the lid to expose the item field.
4. Enter an item.
5. Watch it fall, collide, and become part of the persistent jar.
6. The resident appraises it according to needs, personality, memory, and current plans.
7. The resident chooses an allowed action, speaks, and updates memories.
8. Existing objects create new possible interactions and stories.

## Character model

Every new resident is generated from a stable seed. The seed drives:

- temperament: optimism, patience, warmth, anger tendency, humor;
- cognition: curiosity, caution, resourcefulness, honesty, impulsiveness;
- social stance: trustfulness, attachment, defiance, desire for company;
- values: freedom, comfort, cleanliness, novelty, safety, fairness;
- preferences: several favored and disliked foods, activities, materials, and topics;
- initial situation: memory fragments, current mood, needs, and escape attitude;
- voice identity and conversational style.

These values bias behavior but do not hard-script it. A happy resident can become frightened. A hostile resident can build trust. A comfortable resident may still decide the player is dangerous.

## Needs and consequences

The initial needs model tracks hunger, thirst, energy, safety, comfort, hygiene, social connection, stimulation, and freedom pressure. Health and status effects sit below those needs.

Consequences persist:

- neglect affects health and trust;
- repeated gifts form preferences and expectations;
- insults, broken promises, and dangerous drops become memories;
- mess and damaged objects remain until handled;
- escape work can span many sessions;
- the resident can forgive, bargain, manipulate, or withdraw.

## Interaction philosophy

Items are not connected by a giant list of one-off pairs. Each receives physical properties, semantic tags, and capabilities such as:

- edible, drinkable, toxic, dirty, fragile;
- grabbable, wearable, throwable, bouncy;
- sharp edge, striking tool, lever, container;
- flexible line, adhesive, absorbent, flammable;
- entertainment, comfort, shelter, light source.

Actions require capabilities. “Strike a ball” needs a swingable tool plus a throwable or bouncy target. This makes a baseball bat, hockey stick, branch, or frying pan behave differently but participate in the same family of interactions.

Special authored rules sit on top for memorable combinations and jokes.

## Tone and boundaries

The game supports kindness, neglect, gross-out humor, conflict, and escape tension. The resident is always presented as a fictional adult with agency, not as a child. Graphic sexual content, sexual violence, and photorealistic torture are outside the intended product. Severe content is filtered or represented abstractly. The game should react honestly to cruelty without turning cruelty into the only rewarding path.

## Commercial shape

- Premium base game on Steam.
- Premium or free-demo-plus-full-unlock model on Android, to be decided after retention tests.
- No paid random personality rolls.
- Cloud conversation usage must have quotas and graceful offline/local fallbacks.
- Player saves preserve the resident and jar across sessions.

## Success criteria

The concept succeeds when a new tester:

1. understands the lid gesture without instruction;
2. laughs or reacts emotionally within five minutes;
3. discovers one unprompted object combination;
4. describes the resident as a person using personality words;
5. returns to see what the resident remembers or attempts next.
